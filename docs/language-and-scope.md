# Persian, Iranian, and Persianate

Rang draws from Persian, Iranian, and Persianate art and culture. These terms
overlap, but they do not mean exactly the same thing.

## Persian

Persian refers first to the Persian language, also called Farsi. It can also
describe artistic, literary, and cultural traditions shaped through that
language. The historical connection of the word to Fars is real, but Persia
has also been used more broadly for a much larger cultural and political
world. That history is one reason the word needs context.

In Rang, use *Persian* when a source identifies the language, artwork, or
tradition that way. Many of the current palettes belong in this category.

## Iranian

In Rang, *Iranian* is a broad cultural term for the many communities,
histories, and visual traditions of Iran. It does not mean that every tradition
in Iran is Persian. It is best to name a source as specifically as its own
record allows, whether Persian, Azeri, Kurdish, Luri, Baluchi, Arab, Turkmen,
Gilaki, Mazandarani, or another tradition.

## Persianate

*Persianate* describes cultural worlds shaped by Persian language, literature,
and artistic exchange beyond the borders of present-day Iran. It is not an
ethnic or national label. A work can be Persianate without being Iranian, and
an Iranian work can draw on several traditions at once.

## How Rang uses these terms

Rang welcomes palettes from Persian, Iranian, and Persianate art and culture.
Contributors should describe each source with the most specific cultural,
regional, linguistic, or community attribution that a reliable source supports.
When traditions meet or overlap, describe that relationship instead of forcing
the work into one broad label.

The aim is simple. Rang is for Persian, Iranian, and Persianate art and
culture. It celebrates their connections while respecting the distinct
communities and histories behind each work.

- [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Iran v. Peoples of Iran”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/iran-v1-peoples-survey/) discusses the linguistic and broader geographic uses of *Iranian* and the diversity of peoples and languages in Iran.

- [UNESCO, “Music of the Iranian ethnic groups”](https://ich.unesco.org/en/Decisions/6.COM/13.22) records distinct languages, music, dance, and arts among communities in Iran.
- [Cambridge University Press, “Persianate Pasts, National Presents”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/9EE45D6953C7F8B150F3304E3133659E) discusses Persianate as a cultural and linguistic category that cannot be reduced to one identity.
